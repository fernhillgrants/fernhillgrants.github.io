Why filings

The answers are public. Reading them takes days.

A private foundation's Form 990-PF lists every grant it paid: who received it, how much, and often what for. Many also state, in the same return, whether they accept requests and how to send one.

That makes a filing better evidence than a foundation's mission statement. A funder that gave $3,000 to a food pantry one county over last year is a real prospect. A funder that says it cares about hunger but only gives to organizations it has already chosen is a wasted application.

Grant databases that sort this out cost a few thousand dollars a year. We do the reading for one organization at a time, for a flat fee.

Straight answers

Questions people ask before ordering.

Do you guarantee we'll get a grant?

No. Nobody honestly can. You get research that shows where a request has a real chance and how to make it.

Who is this for?

Small US nonprofits, roughly $75,000 to $1.5 million a year, with no development staff and no budget for a grants database.

Do you write the applications?

No. This is research only. The report tells you what each funder asks for so the writing goes faster.

Where does the information come from?

IRS Form 990-PF returns, as published by ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer and the GivingTuesday 990 data lake, compared with a funder's own website where it has one.

Is AI involved?

Yes. The research is AI-assisted, and every figure in a report links to the filing it came from so you can check it yourself.

What if a funder's details have changed?

Contacts and deadlines do change. The report flags anything we could not confirm. Check with the funder before you apply.

What if something in our report is wrong?

Reply to your delivery email and tell us. We'll look at it and correct it.
